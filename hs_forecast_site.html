Seeing the disease between the visits

Hidradenitis suppurativa flares come and go, but clinics only score IHS4 at some visits. We forecast each patient’s true average IHS4 for January to June 2026 by modelling what happens in the gaps.

How the model works

Real-world HS records are incomplete by design: scores are missing at a third of visits, patients vanish for months, and flares treated at home never reach the chart. We treat those gaps as part of the signal, not noise to throw away. Every input is computed from data dated on or before 31 December 2025.

  1. Denoise the score history

    Each recorded IHS4 is a noisy reading of a hidden severity level. A Kalman filter tracks that hidden level across irregular gaps; its uncertainty grows the longer a patient goes unscored. On its own this beats carrying forward recent scores.

    severityt = severityt−1 + drift,   score = severity + measurement noise

  2. Look for flares that left no score

    A flare managed outside a scored visit still leaves traces: flare-reactive visits, oral antibiotic courses, incision and drainage, unscored visits. We count and time these to estimate activity the scores missed.

  3. Fix the treatment state

    Treatment at the end of 2025 stays in force through the forecast window, so we describe it precisely: which biologic, how long the patient has been on it, how many lines came before, and how scores changed since it started.

  4. Separate clinical signal from recording habits

    Practitioners differ in how often and how high they score. We summarise each practitioner’s documentation pattern without using any outcomes, then test whether those features help or just encode recording artefacts.

  5. Predict the median

    A gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) with an absolute-error objective, because the median is what minimises the competition metric. We validate with random 5-fold splits and with a held-out demographic group to probe distribution shift.

What we learned

Baseline Hurley stage carries information the score history doesn’t. Removing demographics hurts most, almost entirely through Hurley stage, which records structural damage that IHS4 counts can miss.

Hidden-flare proxies earn their place. Antibiotic courses, flare visits and surgeries reduce error even with the full score history present.

Practitioner features slightly hurt. That suggests they mostly encode how practitioners record, not how patients fare: a recording artefact the model is better off ignoring.

Ethnicity is not the shift. Holding out each ethnic group in turn changed error by under 0.02, so the test_2 gap must come from something else. Our adversarial check will identify which features differ once test_2 is scored.

Privacy and the path to deployment

This prototype is built so that patient data never has to leave the clinician’s device: the model and all feature calculations run in the browser, and the demo stores nothing beyond the sign-in session for the current tab.

The sign-in shown here is a placeholder for the interface, not real security. A deployed version would need, at minimum:

Identity. Hospital single sign-on with multi-factor authentication, and role-based access so clinicians see only their own patients.

Data protection. GDPR-compliant processing, data minimisation (only the fields the model uses), encryption in transit and at rest, and audit logs of every prediction viewed.

Integration. Reading histories directly from the electronic health record instead of manual entry, which also removes transcription errors.

Validation and regulation. Prospective validation on real patients across sites, monitoring for drift as practice changes, and conformity assessment as software as a medical device under the EU MDR before any clinical use.
